A drilled well is the default water in rural Bonner County. The Idaho Department of Water Resources keeps Well Driller’s Reports (logs) from roughly July 1987 forward.

Search: IDWR well construction research.

Search order that works

  1. Well tag if you can see it: metal plate on the casing, D plus seven digits (example D0012345). Best match.
  2. Else Township, Range, Section, quarter-quarter. Same language as the septic search. Get it from the deed, assessor, or GIS — not from the listing paragraph.

The log should show depth, static water, geology, construction, and a flow number. Ask for a recent pump test anyway. A 1989 log is a start, not a promise.

How to read GPM without romance

Flow Plain talk
4+ GPM Often acceptable for a house with storage
8+ GPM Comfortable household
15+ GPM Strong

Season, storage tank, and a jet pump vs submersible change how that number feels at the tap. E. coli / coliform is a lab, not a vibe. Shared wells and community systems fail in public records around here — search the neighbors.

Cost planning

Local planning ranges for a new well around Sandpoint are often $20,000–$40,000+ depending on depth and rock. “Wells are good in the area” is marketing.

Logs are public. Confirmation is a licensed driller and a water test you pay for during inspection, before you close.
